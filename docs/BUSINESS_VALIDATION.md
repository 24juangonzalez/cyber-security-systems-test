# Business Validation

- **Status:** Active discovery plan
- **Owner:** Founders
- **Last updated:** 2026-09-16
- **Review cadence:** Weekly

## Objective

Determine whether the prototype solves a recurring, expensive problem for a
reachable buyer and whether the analysis can be delivered safely and
repeatably.

## Leading customer hypothesis

A 50–500-person manufacturer, warehouse, or logistics operator that depends
on third-party remote access, has limited dedicated cybersecurity staffing,
and cannot clearly explain how external or privileged identities could reach
operationally sensitive systems.

## User hypotheses

- IT manager or infrastructure administrator
- Automation engineer
- Systems integrator
- MSP analyst
- Security consultant

## Buyer hypotheses

- Owner or general manager
- IT director
- Operations director
- Integrator or MSP owner
- Risk or compliance leader
- Executive responding to insurance requirements

## Buying triggers to test

- Cyber-insurance renewal
- Customer security questionnaire
- New equipment or automation deployment
- Commissioning or remote-support setup
- Expansion of vendor access
- Security incident
- Audit
- New site or acquisition
- Ransomware event in the industry

## Discovery groups

The first round should include integrators, industrial IT or automation
professionals, MSP or security consultants, and insurance or risk
professionals. Show the prototype report instead of describing an imaginary
platform.

## Questions to answer

- Does the path resemble a real situation?
- Which evidence is missing or misleading?
- Who owns remediation?
- Who approves spending?
- What event creates urgency?
- Which input could be supplied safely?
- Which output fits the current workflow?
- How often does the underlying information change?
- Would the customer buy an assessment, tool, recurring service, or partner
  license?
- What would prevent a pilot?

## Commitment evidence

Rank observed evidence from weak to strong:

1. Positive reaction
2. Follow-up conversation
3. Introduction to the user or buyer
4. Offer of sanitized data
5. Prototype review with additional stakeholders
6. Written design-partner interest
7. Acceptance of a pilot scope
8. Acceptance of a price or purchasing process

Product direction should be chosen from commitments, not compliments.

## Phase 1 continuation criteria

- Five qualified prospects understand the report.
- Three recognize the scenario as materially similar to a real problem.
- Three would act on a comparable finding.
- Two provide realistic follow-up, data, or design feedback.
- One credible buyer shows pilot interest.

## Failure or change signals

- Prospects do not recognize the modeled problem.
- Required data cannot be obtained safely.
- Findings do not affect remediation decisions.
- The user and buyer cannot be identified.
- The workflow is purely one-time and cannot support an attractive offer.
- Existing tools already solve the problem satisfactorily at an acceptable
  price.

## Interview data handling

Keep raw notes and recordings in a restricted system. Commit only sanitized,
permissioned summaries. Do not store personal information, customer network
details, contractual information, or customer exports in this repository.
