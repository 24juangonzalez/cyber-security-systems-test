# Go to Market Hypotheses

- **Status:** Proposed and unvalidated
- **Owner:** Founders
- **Last updated:** 2026-09-16
- **Review date:** After the first design-partner agreement

## Initial offer

Offer a safe remote-access and identity-path assessment that shows which
external or privileged identities could potentially reach operationally
sensitive systems, the evidence supporting each path, and the smallest changes
that reduce exposure.

## Initial deliverables

- Executive summary
- Evidence-backed access-path report
- Technical evidence appendix
- Prioritized remediation plan
- Review meeting
- Remediation verification report

## Channels to compare

### Direct to operator

Provides direct learning but may involve longer trust and purchasing cycles.

### Systems integrators

Integrators may already understand remote access, commissioning, and customer
architecture. They could bundle the analysis into existing projects.

### MSPs and security consultants

Partners may provide repeatable access to multiple customers and use the tool
as an analyst multiplier.

### Insurance influenced

Brokers or risk professionals may clarify current underwriting questions and
create a buying trigger, but the product should not claim insurer approval
without evidence.

## Business models to test

- Fixed-fee assessment
- Assessment plus verification
- Annual or quarterly reassessment
- Per-site subscription
- Per-managed-customer partner license
- Hosted recurring analysis

Pricing remains a hypothesis until a real buyer reviews a defined offer.

## Positioning boundaries

The product is not a SOC, SIEM, EDR, vulnerability scanner, pentest, compliance
certification, or guarantee of security. It is an evidence and prioritization
layer for authorized access relationships.

## Distribution decision

The winning channel should minimize customer-acquisition friction, preserve
trust, enable safe evidence access, and produce repeatable use. Choose it after
prototype demonstrations and pilot discussions.
