# Roadmap

- **Status:** Active planning baseline
- **Owner:** Founders
- **Last updated:** 2026-09-16
- **Review cadence:** Weekly

## Ninety day outcome

Within ninety days, produce a trustworthy prototype, demonstrate it to
qualified prospects, secure one design partner, run one controlled evaluation,
and collect credible evidence about the buyer, workflow, first integration,
and commercial model.

## Phase 1 Foundation

**Target:** Weeks 1 and 2

### Technical outcome

- Provider-neutral domain model
- Validated JSON fixtures
- Vulnerable and remediated industrial scenarios
- Graph construction
- One path rule and one remediation rule
- JSON and Markdown or HTML reports
- CLI and known-answer tests

### Business outcome

- Contact three integrators
- Contact three industrial IT or automation professionals
- Contact two MSP or security consultants
- Contact two insurance or risk professionals
- Schedule at least five prototype-review conversations

### Gate

Continue when the prototype detects and resolves the known path, five prospects
understand the report, and three recognize the scenario as materially similar
to a real problem.

## Phase 2 Useful Output

**Target:** Weeks 3 and 4

### Technical outcome

- Stable finding identifiers
- Confidence and completeness classifications
- Preconditions and uncertainty
- Remediation-edge mapping
- Before-and-after comparison
- Secondary AWS fixture
- Five-minute demonstration

### Business outcome

- Eight to ten qualified demonstrations
- Evidence about user, buyer, trigger, report format, safe inputs, recurrence,
  and purchase process
- At least two candidates for continued design feedback

### Gate

Proceed toward a pilot when five prospects understand the report, three would
act on a similar finding, two offer realistic follow-up or data, and one
credible buyer shows pilot interest.

## Phase 3 Design Partner Adapter

**Target:** Weeks 5 through 8

### Technical outcome

- One evidence-selected import adapter or read-only collector
- Provenance and schema validation
- Duplicate and conflict handling
- Partial-data and denied-access behavior
- Sanitization and retention controls
- Customer-specific criticality and zone definitions
- Audit trail

### Business outcome

- Written pilot scope
- Named participants
- Authorized data and systems
- Explicit exclusions
- Success criteria
- Feedback cadence
- Free or paid terms

### Gate

Begin a pilot only when the customer accepts scope and data handling, the
required evidence can be obtained safely, and both parties agree how success
will be measured.

## Phase 4 Controlled Pilot

**Target:** Weeks 9 through 12

### Workflow

```text
Authorize scope
→ import evidence
→ normalize and analyze
→ manually verify
→ review with customer
→ customer remediates
→ rerun
→ verify path removal
→ collect purchase evidence
```

### Measures

- Onboarding and analysis time
- Collection completeness
- Supported versus unsupported paths
- Findings acted upon
- Time to remediation
- Successful verification
- Requested recurrence
- Budget owner and purchasing objections
- Integrator repeatability

### Final gate

Choose whether to continue, narrow, change market, change channel, or stop. A
successful outcome includes one trusted and actionable path, safe operation,
verified remediation, and credible demand for continued use.

## Weekly cadence

Each week has one technical outcome, one business-learning objective, one
owner for each, and explicit success criteria. At the end of the week, review
what was built, what was demonstrated, which assumptions changed, what
commitments were received, and which risk is now highest.
